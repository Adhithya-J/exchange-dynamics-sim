How to read The Commons

This page replays the simulation. It does not change the rules or the numbers. Each villager is an agent from the recorded run.

  1. Huts and villagers. Every villager has a hut on the ring around the central square. The roof color matches the villager. All villagers are the same size.
  2. Coin boxes are money. A fixed box beside each hut fills with gold coins as its owner holds more, and empties as they give, spend, or pay the living cost. The box is the money indicator, not the body.
  3. The commons. The grassy outer ring of berry bushes and ponds is the village's shared natural stock. As the percentage falls, the bushes dry and thin out and the ponds recede. The percentage in the header and on the meadow is what is left.
  4. Exchanges. A gold coin is money changing hands. Gifts and repayments happen at the receiver's hut. Market trades happen in the central square, where coins and goods cross.
  5. Death. A villager at the resource floor is replaced by a tombstone; their hut disappears and they stop taking part.
  6. Explore. Press space to play or pause, use the arrows to step, drag the timeline to scrub, and click a villager or a feed row to inspect them.

Each iteration: gifts and repayments, then production and trade if the market is enabled, then consumption, then the living cost for every living villager.
