How to read Subsistence

This page replays the simulation. It does not change the rules or the numbers. Every villager is an agent from the recorded run.

  1. Health is a food buffer. Full health survives only four days without food. A day with a full ration restores one point; a day short of food drains it. Missing the six-berry ration creates immediate scarcity.
  2. Berries are food. A villager eats every iteration. Berries outside a storage crate spoil at the end of the iteration.
  3. Crates are capital. Eight raw wood plus about one day of skilled carpentry make a crate, which stores ten berries. Raw wood is material, not storage. Crates are perishable and wear faster when full. Villagers keep a small food reserve and one spare crate's worth of raw wood instead of filling every crate.
  4. Prices are discovered. Every villager has an integer price belief in berries per wood. Bilateral bargains clear at the integer midpoint of seller and buyer reservations; gossip updates beliefs with a one-tick lag. There is no global price. Choices also carry a small daily taste shock, so villagers occasionally try the other activity.
  5. Credit is optional. A loan moves wood, berries, or crate capacity now for a promise of the same good later. Reciprocal credit charges no interest and relies on trust or collateral. Market credit prices the borrower's default risk into a goods-denominated interest rate. A missed payment first uses a grace period, then seizes pledged crates and the borrower's stock.
  6. Gifts can be reciprocal. A plain gift is one-way. With reciprocity enabled it also creates a soft zero-interest obligation that the receiver repays when it has berries above its meal. That is the low-commitment end of the credit spectrum.
  7. Shocks are optional. A drought temporarily reduces berry and wood regeneration, so the same labour yields less until it passes.
  8. Sparse commons costs time. Gathering and chopping minutes rise as bushes and trees thin. Villagers walk between several sparse sites, and the production possibilities frontier contracts with the harvest density shown in the inspector.
  9. Skills differ. Berry skill and wood skill set each villager's production possibilities frontier. A steep frontier means a villager gives up a lot of berries to chop one wood.
  10. Barter is relative. There is no money. The header shows the realized market price; the inspector shows each villager's belief and the PPF uses that belief for its price line.
  11. The commons is shared. Berry bushes and forest thin when everyone gathers and recover as they regenerate. A depleted commons limits everyone.
  12. Explore. Press space to play or pause, use the arrows to step, drag the timeline to scrub, click a villager, and press P or the PPF button to expand the production possibilities chart.

Iteration order: optional shock roll, repay due credit and soft gifts, regenerate the commons, allocate the day across berries, wood, and carpentry, optional gifts, bilateral barter, originate new credit, eat, then crate wear and berry spoilage. Berries, wood, and crates are always whole units.
